Make a live style

A live style is a moving background for a group in VesperTV's TV tab: snow falling, stars twinkling, a puck sliding by. It sits still while you're elsewhere and moves while the remote is on the group. A style is a small text file (JSON) you can write yourself, share with friends, or have an AI make. It can also hold little pixel characters that run, hop and slide along the row: draw them in the scene editor.

Four steps

1Start from the starter

Copy the file below (or download it). It has two styles to change.

2Make it yours

Change the colours, shapes and motions with the lists further down. Give it your own id and name.

3Bring it to the TV

Settings > Appearance > Look > Your live styles. Paste a link to the file (a GitHub Gist's Raw link works), or press From phone and paste the text itself on your phone.

4Put it on a group

Hold OK on a group > Colour… > Live styles… > Your styles. It follows your other devices with sync.

Or ask your AI

With an AI app connected to VesperTV (Vesper AI), just describe it:

Make a live style with orange leaves falling slowly and put it on my Movies group.

Make a scene with a little robot rolling along my Kids group and waving at the end.

The AI writes the style, imports it and puts it on the group. Reconnect the connector once after updating VesperTV so the AI sees the new live_styles tool.

The starter file

A pack: {"styles": [ ... ]}. One style on its own works too.

{
  "version": 1,
  "styles": [
    {
      "id": "autumn-leaves",
      "name": {"en": "Autumn leaves", "fr": "Feuilles d'automne"},
      "fade": ["#99B4541A", "#4D7A2E0E", "#00000000"],
      "layers": [
        {"shape": "glyph:eco", "motion": "fall", "count": 9, "size": [8, 13], "speed": 12,
         "color": ["#E8892B", "#C8501E", "#F2C14E"], "opacity": [0.6, 1], "spin": 0.25, "sway": 8}
      ]
    },
    {
      "id": "night-sky",
      "name": "Night sky",
      "fade": ["#99101A3A", "#4D0A1028", "#00000000"],
      "pulse": 0.2,
      "layers": [
        {"shape": "star4", "motion": "twinkle", "count": 14, "size": [2, 5], "speed": 0.6,
         "color": ["#FFE9A8", "#FFFFFF"], "opacity": [0.3, 1]},
        {"shape": "star4", "motion": "streak", "count": 1, "size": 7, "speed": 150, "gap": 4,
         "color": "#FFD54F", "opacity": 1, "glow": true, "y": [0.1, 0.5]}
      ]
    }
  ]
}

A style

id

Letters, numbers and dashes ("autumn-leaves"). Import the same id again to update the style. VesperTV puts "my-" in front of it.

name

A name, or one per language: {"en": "Autumn leaves", "fr": "Feuilles d'automne"}.

fade

Colours behind the group, left to right, as #AARRGGBB (AA = opacity, 00 clear to FF solid). End with #00000000 so the group's name and count stay easy to read.

pulse

Optional: the fade breathes, in cycles a second (0.2 is gentle; up to 4).

layers

Up to a few sets of particles (below).

scene

Optional: pixel characters on a timeline. A style needs a fade, a layer or a scene (any mix of them).

engine

Set it to 2 when the style has a scene: an older VesperTV then leaves the style out instead of drawing it without its characters.

A layer

shape

What is drawn (list below).

motion

How it moves (list below).

count

How many, 1-40 (default 10). 6-16 looks best on a row.

size

Pixels: one number, or [smallest, largest]. A row is about 260 x 44 px.

speed

Pixels a second for moving motions; cycles a second for twinkle, chase and wave.

color

"#RRGGBB" (or #AARRGGBB), or a list: each particle picks one.

opacity

[lowest, highest] between 0 and 1, or one number.

spin

Turns a second (negative turns the other way).

sway

Pixels of side-to-side swing (fall, rise, drift); a wave's height.

tilt

Pixels sideways for each pixel down (slanted rain).

gap

Seconds between passes (streak, slide, arc, burst).

glow

true = a soft glow around each particle.

fadeOut

true = fades away along its way (embers).

x / y

The part of the row it keeps to, as fractions: [0, 0.5] = the left half / the top half.

chars

For shape "char": the characters it picks from ("01").

Motions

fall

falls down the row, swaying with "sway"

rise

rises (bubbles, embers)

drift

floats left to right

drift-left

floats right to left

twinkle

stays in place, brightness pulses ("speed" = cycles a second)

chase

evenly spaced lights, a bright one runs along (Christmas or cinema lights)

streak

a shooting star with a tail, then a pause of "gap" seconds

bounce

bounces along the bottom

roll

rolls along the bottom, turning

slide

a fast pass with a short trail (a puck)

arc

thrown in an arc (a football)

burst

fireworks

still

does not move (rink or pitch lines)

wave

soft bands rolling ("sway" = height)

Scenes: little characters

A scene is pixel art on a timeline: a fox that runs and jumps over a log, a rocket that flies by. While the remote stays on the group it loops from its rest moment (about 30 s, in whole loops); when the remote leaves, it's back at its rest moment. On a phone it plays for about 12 s when the row shows. Needs VesperTV 1.0.74 or newer.

The easy way: the scene editor. Draw each sprite pixel by pixel, give it a path, watch the row play the way your TV will, then copy the file. Start from a blank scene or from one of the built-in ones. Draw your own characters, not known ones or logos, so your styles can be shared freely.

A small one to start from: a ball bouncing across the row.

{
  "id": "bouncy-ball",
  "name": "Bouncy ball",
  "engine": 2,
  "fade": ["#991A237E", "#4D1A237E", "#00000000"],
  "scene": {
    "length": 6,
    "rest": 3,
    "grid": 18,
    "sprites": {
      "ball": {
        "palette": {"r": "#E53935", "w": "#FFFFFF", "d": "#9E1E1B"},
        "frames": [[".rrr.", "rwrrr", "rrrrd", "rrrdd", ".ddd."]]
      }
    },
    "actors": [
      {
        "sprite": "ball",
        "path": [
          {"t": 0, "x": -0.05, "y": 1, "arc": 0.6},
          {"t": 1.5, "x": 0.3, "y": 1, "arc": 0.45},
          {"t": 3, "x": 0.6, "y": 1, "arc": 0.3},
          {"t": 4.5, "x": 0.85, "y": 1, "arc": 0.2},
          {"t": 6, "x": 1.1, "y": 1}
        ]
      }
    ]
  }
}
length

Seconds in one loop (0.5 to 60; 6 to 10 works well).

rest

The second shown while the group is still. Pick a moment with the main character between the middle and 80 % across: the name is on the left, the count on the right.

grid

How many sprite pixels tall the row is (8 to 64). 16 to 22 suits most rows: smaller = bigger characters.

sprites

Up to 12 characters or props, each with a name: {"fox": {...}, "log": {...}}.

palette

A sprite's colours, one character each: {"o": "#E8892B", "k": "#2E2118"} (up to 24). . and a space are always clear.

frames

Pictures made of rows of palette characters, up to 32 x 32 (8 to 16 rows tall reads best). One list of frames, or named sets: {"run": [[rows], [rows]], "jump": [[rows]]} (up to 6 sets of 8 frames).

fps

Frames a second for the sprite (0 to 24, 6 by default).

actors

Up to 16 sprites on the row; later ones are drawn in front. Still: {"sprite": "log", "at": [0.55, 1]}. Moving: {"sprite": "fox", "path": [points]}.

path

Points in time (up to 48): {"t": 2.6, "x": 0.46, "y": 1, "play": "jump", "arc": 0.5}. Between two points the sprite moves in a straight line; before the first and after the last it stays put.

x, y

x across the row (0 left, 1 right) is the sprite's middle; y down the row (0 top, 1 bottom) is its feet. Below 0 or above 1 = off the row, for coming in and going out.

play, flip

From this point on: the frame set to play, and "flip": true to face left (false faces right again).

arc, ease

To the next point: a hop as high as arc rows (0.5 = half the row), and ease in (slow start), out (slow end), inout or linear.

show

Optional: only part of the loop, [1, 4] seconds, or several: [[0, 2], [5, 6]]. [7, 1] crosses the loop's end.

Tips

  • A group row is about 260 x 44 pixels: 1 to 3 layers of 6 to 16 particles look best.
  • Keep the fade soft (about 60 % at the left, 99 in #AARRGGBB) and end it clear so the group's name stays readable.
  • A layer with a shape or motion VesperTV doesn't know is left out; a style with nothing left isn't imported. Import tells you how many were left out.
  • All 29 built-in styles are in group-styles.json: borrow from them. The scene editor opens the built-in scenes too.
  • A scene's sprite or actor VesperTV can't draw (a missing sprite, a frame set it doesn't have) is left out; the scene editor lists what and why.
  • A link is downloaded again when the gallery opens (once each time VesperTV starts), so changes to your file reach the TV by themselves.
